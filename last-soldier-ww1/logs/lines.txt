At 10:59 in the morning, an American soldier charged straight at a German machine gun.
The war would end in sixty seconds.
It was November the eleventh, 1918. At five that morning, the armistice had been signed in a train carriage in France.
The guns would stop at eleven. Until then, the fighting went on.
Nearly three thousand men died on the last day of the war.
His name was Henry Gunther, from Baltimore. Weeks earlier, he had been demoted for writing a letter home about how bad the war really was.
Witnesses said the German soldiers knew the war was ending. They waved at him to go back.
He kept running. They fired. He died at 10:59.
One minute later, the war was over.
The army later gave him back his rank. Every November the eleventh, the world remembers. Follow for the story behind the news.
