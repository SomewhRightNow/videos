In nineteen sixty-nine, a football match started a real war.
Honduras and El Salvador. Two neighbours, one ticket to the World Cup.
About three hundred thousand Salvadorans lived and farmed in Honduras.
That year, Honduras began forcing them off the land.
Then came the games. In Tegucigalpa, Honduras won one-nil.
In San Salvador, El Salvador won three-nil.
The playoff went to Mexico City. El Salvador won three-two, in extra time.
That same day, El Salvador cut all ties with Honduras.
Less than three weeks later, its army crossed the border.
In the sky, World War Two fighter planes fought the last propeller dogfights in history.
The war lasted about one hundred hours. Thousands died, most of them civilians.
And at the World Cup, El Salvador lost all three games, without scoring a single goal.
