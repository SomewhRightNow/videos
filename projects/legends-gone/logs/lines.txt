On a Friday night in Buenos Aires, Argentina played their first game without Lionel Messi.
Twenty-four hours later, in Braga, Portugal played their first game without Cristiano Ronaldo.
Both of them lost.
For twenty years, these two men carried their countries. This is the story of the weekend football had to learn to live without them.
Chapter one. The two kings.
Cristiano Ronaldo first played for Portugal in August two thousand and three. He was eighteen.
Two years later, Lionel Messi made his debut for Argentina. It lasted about two minutes. He came on, pushed an opponent away, and was sent off.
From those beginnings came the greatest rivalry the sport has ever seen.
Ronaldo became the top scorer in the history of men's international football. One hundred and forty-six goals, in two hundred and thirty-four games.
He won the European Championship in twenty sixteen, and the Nations League twice.
Messi won almost everything else. Two Copa Américas. And in twenty twenty-two, in Qatar, the World Cup.
Eight Ballon d'Ors for Messi. Five for Ronaldo.
Chapter two. The goodbyes.
Their final summer was the twenty twenty-six World Cup.
Argentina reached the final. Spain won it, one-nil, in extra time. Messi walked up to collect his runner-up medal in tears.
Weeks later, he wrote a note by hand. He was retiring from the national team. A decision, he wrote, that hurt deep in his soul.
Ronaldo's ending was not so quiet. In the autumn, he was left on the bench against Norway, and walked out of the squad.
The federation suspended him for thirty days. On the fifth of November, he announced his retirement. Twenty-three years were over.
Chapter three. Friday, Buenos Aires.
The thirteenth of November. La Bombonera. Argentina against Uruguay, and their new coach, Diego Forlán.
Only around twenty-five thousand fans came. Less than half of the stadium.
Forty-eight minutes. An own goal by Leonardo Balerdi. Fifty-three. Maxi Araujo. Two-nil.
Julian Alvarez scored with seventeen minutes left. It was not enough. Uruguay won, two-one.
We have to rebuild without Leo Messi, said coach Lionel Scaloni.
Chapter four. Saturday, Braga.
The next night, we were in the stadium in Braga.
Before kick-off, the Portugal players walked out wearing the number seven. The stadium said goodbye. Obrigado, Capitao.
Then Denmark ruined the party. Pierre-Emile Hojbjerg scored a penalty just before half-time.
Rasmus Hoylund scored twice. Joachim Andersen added a fourth.
Bernardo Silva scored in stoppage time. It changed nothing. Portugal one, Denmark four.
It was the heaviest competitive home defeat in Portugal's history.
Chapter five. What now?
Two legends gone. Two defeats in twenty-four hours.
But history has a warning. In the Euro twenty sixteen final, Ronaldo went off injured after twenty-five minutes. Portugal still beat France.
Argentina still have Julian Alvarez, Enzo Fernandez and Alexis Mac Allister. Portugal have Vitinha, Joao Neves, Bruno Fernandes and Rafael Leao.
The question is no longer who is better, Messi or Ronaldo. The question is who can follow them.
